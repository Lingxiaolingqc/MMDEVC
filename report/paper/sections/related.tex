\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Geometry and benchmarks.}
Semi-Global Matching (SGM) approximates a stereo energy through pathwise cost aggregation and addresses correspondence, smoothness, and occlusion handling~\cite{sgm}. We use OpenCV's modified StereoSGBM implementation~\cite{opencv}, so this is not an exact reimplementation of every component of the original paper. Middlebury provides calibrated stereo imagery and disparity ground truth~\cite{middlebury}. KITTI broadens evaluation to driving scenes and multiple geometric tasks~\cite{kitti}; its relevance here is the need to examine behavior outside controlled scenes, not an additional dataset used in our experiments.

\paragraph{Monocular foundations.}
Dense Prediction Transformers (DPT) reassemble transformer features into spatial representations and decode dense outputs~\cite{dpt}. Depth Anything emphasizes large-scale unlabeled-image supervision~\cite{dav1}. Depth Anything V2 improves the teacher/student training recipe using synthetic supervision and pseudo-labeled real images, and releases both relative and metric variants~\cite{dav2}. We keep these representations separate: the relative checkpoint supplies inverse-depth-like structure, while the Hypersim metric checkpoint produces depth in metres. GT-assisted alignment evaluates relative structure but does not turn that model into a deployable metric estimator.

\paragraph{Metric depth.}
UniDepth predicts metric 3D structure while conditioning depth on a learned camera representation~\cite{unidepth}. UniDepthV2 revises the architecture and adds edge-oriented training and an uncertainty output~\cite{unidepthv2}. Our ViT-S experiment uses predicted intrinsics and the official returned Z-depth map; we do not supply GT intrinsics or exploit its uncertainty output in fusion. Depth Pro combines a multi-scale transformer, boundary-sensitive prediction, and focal-length estimation~\cite{depthpro}. It motivates inspecting boundaries separately from global errors, but is reviewed rather than executed in our Small-first study.

\paragraph{Motion and video depth.}
RAFT estimates optical flow using all-pairs correlations and recurrent refinement~\cite{raft}. We instead use the less expensive Farneb\"ack method, which estimates displacement from local polynomial expansions~\cite{farneback}. This choice limits the reliability of correspondences in low texture, reflections, and large motion. VDA builds on Depth Anything V2 with a temporal head and temporal training objectives~\cite{vda}. We preserve its official offline inference window and compare it with a causal postprocessor; their throughput measurements must not be interpreted as equivalent online latency. Our adaptive fusion is a transparent heuristic whose benefits require controlled ablation, rather than a calibrated probabilistic confidence model.
