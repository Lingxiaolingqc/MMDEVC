\subsection{Calibrated StereoSGBM}
Disparity is horizontal correspondence displacement in pixels. Given focal length $f_{\rm px}$, baseline $B_{\rm mm}$, and principal-point offset $d_{\rm offs}$ from Middlebury calibration, we compute camera-axis depth as
\begin{equation}
Z_{\rm m}=\frac{f_{\rm px}B_{\rm mm}}{1000(d_{\rm px}+d_{\rm offs})}.
\label{eq:stereo}
\end{equation}
OpenCV returns fixed-point disparities, divided by 16 before conversion. Invalid sentinels are rejected. The search bound uses the calibration's conservative disparity range rather than a tight bound inferred from GT values.

A separate right-to-left match checks consistency. At corresponding coordinates, the signed left and right disparities should sum to approximately zero. Out-of-bounds coordinates, unavailable right predictions, or residuals above 1 pixel are rejected. The final mask also requires positive finite $Z$; holes are not filled. A zero disparity can remain geometrically valid when $d_{\rm offs}>0$. We select grayscale block size 3 and penalties $P_1=72$, $P_2=288$ on two development scenes from block sizes 5, 3, and 7. All 15 final pairs use this fixed configuration.

\subsection{Pretrained models and output meaning}
UniDepthV2 ViT-S receives only RGB through the released \texttt{infer(rgb)} interface. We use its camera-axis Z output in metres, with predicted camera parameters and no GT scale fitting. The actual encoder image is $434\times574$; the official pipeline restores the output to $480\times640$.

For video, DA V2 Small and VDA Small both use their \emph{relative} checkpoints. Their values are inverse-depth-like: larger values generally indicate nearer content, but numerical magnitudes are arbitrary and the two models need not share a scale. Finite zero and negative values are not rejected merely for being nonpositive. No inverse conversion to metres or GT alignment is performed. VDA retains official offline window processing and relative window stitching. We do not train or fine-tune any model.

\subsection{One improvement: flow-guided fusion}
Let $r_t$ be current raw relative output and $\widehat r_{t-1}$ the previous fused map. Forward flow $f_t$ maps previous to current pixels; backward flow $b_t$ maps current to previous pixels. For current coordinate $p$,
\begin{equation}
q=p+b_t(p),\qquad h_t(p)=\widehat r_{t-1}(q).
\end{equation}
Bilinear interpolation requires finite supporting values and in-bounds coordinates. RGB Farneb\"ack flow is computed once and reused across variants. We form forward--backward and photometric residuals:
\begin{align}
e_f(p)&=\|b_t(p)+f_t(q)\|_2,\\
e_p(p)&=\|I_t(p)-I_{t-1}(q)\|_1/(3\cdot255).
\end{align}
Correspondences pass the consistency filter when
\begin{align}
e_f^2&\leq0.01\big(\|b_t\|_2^2+\|f_t(q)\|_2^2\big)+0.5,\\
e_p&\leq0.2.
\end{align}
These checks estimate correspondence reliability; they are not true occlusion labels. Disocclusions and reflections can violate their assumptions.

The fusion update is
\begin{equation}
\widehat r_t=(1-w_t)r_t+w_th_t.
\label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
For E4, define a current-frame robust span $s_t=Q_{.99}(r_t)-Q_{.01}(r_t)$ over finite values. Let $e_d=|h_t-r_t|/s_t$, and let $e_e$ be the maximum absolute difference to a four-connected current neighbor, divided by $s_t$. On eligible pixels,
\begin{equation}
w_t=\alpha\exp\left[-e_p/0.1-e_f/1-e_d/0.1-e_e/0.1\right].
\label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
The flow scale is in pixels; the other terms are dimensionless. Elsewhere $w_t=0$. A near-zero span disables history. The first frame is unchanged, and invalid current predictions stay invalid. Importantly, $s_t$ only scales the confidence calculation: it does not normalize the output, align frames, or use future information. A common positive affine transform of the input sequence leaves these confidence terms unchanged apart from numerical precision.

We freeze $\alpha=0.70$ after comparing 0.50, 0.70, and 0.85 on the separate development clip. This heuristic balances history reuse with disagreement and boundary sensitivity. It cannot correct a wrong current prediction without suitable history, recover detail absent from every frame, or resolve per-frame affine ambiguity. Optical flow transfers image coordinates rather than reconstructing 3D motion; real scene changes can still be incorrectly smoothed.
