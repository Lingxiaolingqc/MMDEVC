\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Data and development separation}
Middlebury Stereo v3 is evaluated at its downloaded quarter resolution~\cite{middlebury}. We process all 15 training pairs. Adirondack and Pipes determine the stereo configuration; the other 13 are held out from our selection, but are not the official hidden benchmark test set. Related illumination/exposure variants remain on the same side of the split. The required 15-pair statistics are therefore descriptive and include development data.

NYUv2 provides 1,449 labeled RGB/depth pairs~\cite{nyu}. We preserve the standard 795 train / 654 test split. Three train-side images are used for integration checks; test images are not used for model or parameter selection. We extract the provided filled depth without additional denoising or filling, verify axis order and metres, and infer from the full RGB image. Saved raw predictions are reused for the revised course evaluation; they are not altered by scoring.

The specified test video is the official VDA \texttt{davis\_rollercoaster.mp4}. \textbf{The updated task requests 100 frames, but the complete official file decodes to only 70 frames at 24 fps.} The pinned source and a fresh upstream download have identical SHA256 values. Following the project owner's explicit decision, we use every consecutive source frame 0--69, with timestamps 0--2.875 s, without duplication or interpolation. This records a source/requirement discrepancy, not an instructor-approved exception.

Development uses frames 0--99 of the separate official \texttt{Tokyo-Walk\_rgb.mp4}, retaining 15 fps and timestamps 0--6.6 s. It is a nighttime street example with people and reflective pavement. The development and test scene groups and source hashes differ. The development footage is an auxiliary supplied example, not a benchmark with depth GT or independently verified physical capture. Neither clip provides GT depth; self-recorded footage is not required for this revised delivery.

\subsection{Stereo and NYUv2 accuracy}
Stereo GT pixels have finite strictly positive disparity. An audit verifies that this is exactly the previously saved all-GT mask for every downloaded pair. For this fixed region $G$ and final prediction support $P$, we report $|G\cap P|/|G|$ and disparity MAE on $G\cap P$. MAE is averaged equally over scenes; coverage is pooled over GT pixels. Nonoccluded-region and conditional Z-depth results remain supplementary.

For NYUv2, predictions must have size $480\times640$. The evaluation region is Python's half-open crop $[45{:}471,41{:}601]$ intersected with finite GT in $[0.001,10]$ m. We clamp finite predictions to this same range. Nonfinite predictions in the region cause evaluation failure instead of being silently excluded. With clipped prediction $p_i$ and GT $g_i$,
\begin{align}
\mathrm{AbsRel}&=|G|^{-1}\sum_{i\in G}|p_i-g_i|/g_i,\\
\mathrm{RMSE}&=\sqrt{|G|^{-1}\sum_{i\in G}(p_i-g_i)^2},\\
\delta_1&=|G|^{-1}\sum_{i\in G}\mathbf{1}\{\max(p_i/g_i,g_i/p_i)<1.25\}.
\end{align}
Each metric is computed per image and then averaged equally across all 654 test images. The strict threshold is retained. The previous unclipped protocol and GT-aligned relative experiments remain in historical result files; their numbers are not mixed into this main table.

\subsection{Video inference and display}
Both video models consume identical cached RGB frames resized with preserved aspect ratio to $640\times360$. Original presentation timestamps and consecutive frames are retained. We first attempted VDA at input short side 518, which failed with CUDA out-of-memory during warmup on this laptop. We reduced both models to 364; their encoder spatial dimensions are $364\times644$. FP16 and official preprocessing are retained. VDA uses its original 32-frame offline windows, overlap, stitching, and internal padding; that internal padding does not add frames to our exported video.

Every relative depth movie uses Viridis with limits fixed over the whole clip. Raw DA V2 determines its 1st and 99th percentile limits, shared unchanged by E0--E4. VDA uses its own whole-clip limits. Colors therefore encode relative order within each method, not a common metre scale. Display saturation does not clip the saved raw array. No per-frame display normalization is used.

The RGB/flow checks define a fixed reference $R_t$ for adjacent frames before scoring any method. Let $V_t$ be its subset with valid current and warped previous predictions. With one fixed span $S$ per reference model, we compute
\begin{equation}
T=\frac{\sum_t\left[\sum_{p\in V_t}\min\left(\frac{|r_t(p)-r_{t-1}(q)|}{S},1\right)+|R_t\setminus V_t|\right]}{\sum_t|R_t|}.
\label{eq:temporal}
\end{equation}
For each variant, $r$ in this diagnostic denotes that variant's saved output. E0--E4 share DA's span; E5 uses VDA's own span. Missing pairs pay the capped penalty 1, and pair coverage is reported. This optional quantity measures normalized change after estimated motion alignment, not depth accuracy. A lower score can result from lag or loss of detail, and comparing independently normalized models does not remove all relative-depth ambiguities.

The three candidate weights are compared only on development data, using $T$, departure from the current raw prediction, and saved contour comparisons. The freeze records the review, candidate verification receipts, source hashes, and selected parameters. Test fusion rejects an absent or inconsistent freeze. With no static-camera sequence or video GT, we do not report static flicker, calibrated response delay, or metric video error.

\subsection{Efficiency and provenance}
Runs use an RTX 4060 Laptop with 8,188 MiB reported memory, PyTorch 2.10.0+cu128, and FP16. Warmup precedes measured inference. CUDA-synchronized inference includes preprocessing, transfers, network execution, output restoration, and VDA stitching; it excludes loading weights, decoding, and disk writes. Peak memory is PyTorch allocated/reserved memory rather than total device occupancy. CPU flow and fusion times are measured separately. Their summed-time throughput is an additive estimate, not measured end-to-end FPS or online latency.

Source commits are pinned for DA V2 (\texttt{a561b849}), VDA (\texttt{4f5ae231}), and UniDepth (\texttt{8d8cfe4c}). Complete revisions, checkpoint hashes, tensor shapes, original output hashes, and timing boundaries accompany each run. Numerical reproducibility is checked from saved arrays independently of network inference.
