\section{Introduction}
Depth supports scene interpretation and image effects, but a plausible color map is not evidence of correct distance. Stereo uses correspondence and calibration; monocular models infer geometry from learned priors. Relative outputs describe structure without necessarily providing metres, which changes both evaluation and downstream use.

Video also separates stability from accuracy. Averaging can suppress noise, but also delays genuine changes at moving silhouettes or approaching objects. A frozen prediction could appear stable while being unusable. Temporal scores therefore need interpretation alongside contours, motion response, and correspondence failures.

Our revised Spring 2027 project connects calibrated StereoSGBM, direct metric UniDepthV2 evaluation on NYUv2, and relative DA V2/VDA video comparison. The single proposed improvement is a causal flow-guided postprocessor for frame-wise DA; VDA remains an independent reference. We preserve raw arrays, timestamps, hashes, and development-selection evidence, allowing results to be recomputed without inference. A background-blur application uses relative ordering without unsupported distance readouts. The short supplied test clip supports a controlled qualitative study rather than broad video-depth generalization.
